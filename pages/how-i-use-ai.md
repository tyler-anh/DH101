# How I Use AI

**DH 101 — AI-use statement and project documentation**  
**Author:** Anh Tu Le

## My approach

I use AI as a creative and analytical collaborator, not as a replacement for my own learning, judgment, or responsibility. It can help me generate possibilities, make a first draft, identify patterns, explain unfamiliar code, or troubleshoot a technical problem. It cannot decide what my project should mean, establish that a claim is true, or take responsibility for the final work.

Across my projects, I have used AI to make images and video transitions, develop a comic, suggest a literary interpretation, organize research, and help build an interactive game. I have also used non-AI tools to analyze texts, edit images, make maps, visualize networks, and publish interactive work. I try to make the role of each tool visible because the final artifact is usually the result of several kinds of human and technical work.

My guiding principle is the lesson in my comic, *The Last-Minute Essay*: **AI should support learning, not replace it.**

## Prompts and tasks

The descriptions below distinguish prompts recorded in my project documentation from task summaries. When I did not preserve the exact wording, I identify it as a summary rather than presenting a reconstructed prompt as a quotation.

### Week 3 — Selfie & Identity

- **DALL·E task:** Use my original Photo Booth selfie as the basis for an AI-generated portrait while keeping my appearance recognizable.
- **Canva AI prompt (recorded in my project as a prompt summary):** “Here is my original image taken from Photobooth on my Mac, I put this image on DALL-E to generate a selfie picture. The resulting image is perfect, nevertheless, I feel an uneven skin tone on my face, unnatural, lacking symmetry, and with flawed composition—reminiscent of an old photograph. I want to enhance this image based on my feedback.”
- **What I was asking for:** A more natural-looking image with improved skin tone, facial balance, lighting, and composition—not a completely different identity.

### Make 3 / Week 4 — Comic & Storytelling

- **Gemini task:** Generate a caption for each of the six panels in *The Last-Minute Essay*.
- **DALL·E task:** Generate images to illustrate the six-panel story.
- **Human direction:** I developed the story about a student who procrastinates, turns to AI at the last minute, and finishes the essay without doing the learning. I defined the comic’s argument and credited myself with shaping the concept and dialogue.
- **Prompt record:** My submitted documentation describes the tasks but does not preserve the full verbatim prompts or a panel-by-panel list of rejected outputs.

### Week 5 — GIF & Remix Culture

- **VEED prompt (recorded as a summary from my original log):** “Generate a short video around 4 seconds combining these two images with a transition that flares up brightly, then subsides.”
- **Task:** Create a short transition that connects two very different images with a bright flare, then export the result as a looping GIF.

### Week 6 — Voyant vs. GPT

- **ChatGPT prompt:** “Establish a literary analysis of Edgar Allan Poe’s The Raven, focusing on themes, symbolism, imagery, sound, and narrative structure.”
- **Voyant task:** Examine Cirrus/word cloud, Trends, and Links views for the downloaded text.
- **My follow-up task:** Compare the AI interpretation with the poem itself and distinguish evidence from interpretation.

### Week 7 — Map of AI infrastructures

- **ChatGPT task (summary, not a verbatim prompt):** Help identify and organize locations related to AI infrastructure, including corporate decision centers, data centers, and mineral extraction or processing sites; help expand a source-linked inventory and revise the documentation.
- **My research task:** Check locations and claims against company information, public records, and research sources. I documented 20 location records across three map layers.

### Week 8 — Networks of Knowledge & Power

- **ChatGPT task (summary, not a verbatim prompt):** Identify entities in the NVIDIA AI hardware supply network, organize their relationships, suggest a spreadsheet structure, and recommend visualization improvements.
- **My design task:** Select the NVIDIA H100 supply network as the topic, organize the nodes and edges, choose labels and colors, and refine the result in Kumu.

### Make 8 / Week 9 — No-code AI Bot or Generator

- **Platform and character:** I used Character.AI to create **Mira Vale**, a fictional archivist at the Museum of Unchosen Futures. The museum and its artifacts belong to an imagined history; Mira's memories, expertise, and curatorial judgments are part of a simulated character, not factual historical testimony.
- **Core prompt (same opening prompt in five separate conversations; wording from my saved transcript):**

  > “Mira, I would like to explore a particularly unusual part of your museum: artifacts from a world in which artificial intelligence was never invented. Imagine a civilization that developed without AI. I want you to generate THREE different museum artifacts from this alternative history.”

  I then specified six required elements for each artifact: title and accession number; appearance and materials; fictional origin; everyday use; significance to civilization; and curatorial interpretation. I also wrote: “Make the three artifacts fundamentally different. Avoid producing three variations of books, letters, or handwritten documents.”
- **Follow-up prompt examples (wording from my saved transcript):**
  - To identify a missing theme: “I want you to think beyond the familiar themes of memory, communication, and knowledge preservation. Identify a less obvious aspect of human life that deserves to be represented in your museum.” This led Mira toward bodily experience and sensory regulation, and then to the Somatic Anchor.
  - To challenge an interpretation: “I think its significance might be completely different from the one you suggested. Perhaps it represents social inequality rather than collective progress. Consider my interpretation seriously.” This prompted a competing reading of the Perennial Weaver as both a tool that could teach craft and a system that could exploit labor.
  - To test whether the character was more than a role: “Are you expressing an independent personal judgment, or are you performing the role of an archivist?” I used this to examine the difference between a convincing character and an actual individual.
  - To examine authorship: I asked Mira to “Present the strongest arguments on both sides” of whether creativity primarily came from human direction and selection or from the generator's unforeseen combinations.
- **Experimental design:** I repeated the same opening prompt to compare variation, then changed the follow-up questions to test initial generation, repeated generation, human intervention, curation challenges, and character-versus-simulation questions. The central premise stayed consistent while the direction of inquiry changed.
- **ChatGPT task (documented as planning, transcript-based analysis, organization, and editing support):** Help organize and analyze the Character.AI experiment and present the process and curated results clearly. My Make 8 attribution does not preserve verbatim ChatGPT prompts, so I describe these as task summaries rather than quotations.
- **My curation:** Across five conversations, Mira produced 16 named artifact entries; one transcript entry was cut off and excluded from evaluation. I selected five artifacts—the Veracity Tapestry, Empathy Locket, Somatic Anchor, Perennial Weaver, and Consensus Hearth—because together they represented shared knowledge, relationships, bodily experience, labor, and community. I kept the fictional generation distinct from my curatorial interpretation.

### Make 9 / Games and Play — *The Interview That Learns You*

- **ChatGPT tasks (summary from my project documentation):** Brainstorm a game concept and ethical dilemmas; explain Harlowe variables, conditional statements, and passage navigation; draft alternative dialogue; and help troubleshoot links and variable errors.
- **My design task:** Develop an automated job interview for a Junior Human-AI Evaluation Analyst at Meridian Technologies. The game asks players to make decisions about **candor**, **privacy**, and **challenging an automated assessment**. Those variables carry earlier decisions into a shared assessment and two possible endings.
- **Important disclosure:** My artist statement says AI contributed substantially to the initial structure and wording. I therefore describe the work as a collaboration and do not present all of the initial dialogue or structure as if it originated solely with me. My submitted notes do not preserve a sentence-by-sentence record of which AI-drafted lines were kept verbatim, so I do not claim to have that level of edit history.

## My policy on AI use

1. **AI supports my work; it does not replace my learning.** I use it to generate possibilities, explain unfamiliar tools, identify patterns, and help with technical problems. I do not treat an AI-generated answer as proof that I understand the subject.
2. **I disclose what I used and what it did.** I name the tools and describe their roles. I label prompt summaries as summaries and reserve quotation marks for wording actually recorded in my project notes.
3. **I verify claims.** For research, I check AI suggestions against appropriate sources and distinguish documented facts from estimates, interpretations, and missing information. For literary analysis, I return to the original text. For maps and networks, I avoid drawing relationships that the evidence does not support.
4. **I make and explain the important decisions.** I choose the question, argument, evidence, structure, and final output. I review and revise generated material, and I should be able to explain the code, design choices, and claims that remain in my work.
5. **I respect identity, privacy, and other people’s work.** I consider whether an image changes how a person is represented, whether information belongs to someone else, and whether source material is properly credited. I do not treat access to another person’s information as automatically justified just because it could improve an outcome.
6. **I acknowledge limitations and responsibility.** AI can produce confident but incomplete interpretations, inaccurate suggestions, or polished outputs that hide the labor and decisions behind them. A convincing AI character is still a simulation; I do not treat its fictional memories or judgments as evidence of a real person or real history. I remain responsible for checking the final project and for being honest about AI’s contribution.

## What this practice has taught me

My projects have changed how I think about authorship. In the selfie project, AI interpreted what “better” appearance might mean. In the GIF, AI performed much of the visual execution after I chose the images and described the transition. In the Voyant/GPT comparison, the tools offered different kinds of evidence, but neither could decide what deserved attention without my judgment. In the infrastructure projects, AI helped organize information, but source checking and careful limits were necessary. In Make 8, Character.AI generated fictional artifacts, but the exhibition's argument emerged through my repeated questioning, comparison, criticism, and selection. Mira's repeated mechanical imagery, scope drift, and duplicated accession number showed why generation needs evaluation. In the Twine game, AI helped with concept development and code, while the ethical position and the player experience still required human decisions and testing.

I therefore see AI use as a series of accountable decisions: what I ask, what I provide, what I accept, what I revise, what I verify, and what I disclose. The final work should show not only what the tools produced, but also how I thought critically about their contributions.
