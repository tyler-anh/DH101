## Reflection
When machines read literature for us, we gain speed, convenience, and new ways to discover patterns that we might overlook. Tools like Voyant can count frequently used words, visualize repetition, and show how language changes throughout a text. ChatGPT can take these patterns further by suggesting interpretations of symbolism, imagery, and themes. For example, when analyzing Edgar Allan Poe’s The Raven, GPT helped me understand how the raven and the repeated word “Nevermore” represent the speaker’s grief and inability to escape painful memories.

However, we also lose something when we rely too heavily on machines. Voyant can count words accurately without understanding their meaning or recognizing whether they are actually relevant to the poem. In my analysis, words related to Project Gutenberg’s copyright information appeared more frequently than meaningful literary words because the text included additional publication information. ChatGPT can offer deeper interpretations, but its confident writing may encourage readers to accept its conclusions without checking the evidence. This experience taught me that reading literature is more than collecting information or finding the “correct” interpretation. Human readers bring personal experiences, emotions, and questions that shape how they understand a poem. Machines can help us notice things we might otherwise miss, but they can also influence what we pay attention to.
