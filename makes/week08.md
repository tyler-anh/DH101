## Process Notes
1. Topic Selection
I chose Option B, AI Supply Chain Network, to explore how companies, materials, manufacturing technologies, and cloud services connect to produce AI computing hardware. I focused on NVIDIA's H100 GPU supply network.

2. Data Collection
I organized the network into nodes and edges using Excel. The nodes represent companies, countries, materials, components, and technologies. The edges describe relationships such as supplying memory, manufacturing chips, providing equipment, and deploying GPUs in cloud services. I used company reports and official technical documentation to research these connections.

3. Building the Visualization
I imported the data into Kumu. When the Excel import did not work, I separated the data into CSV files for elements and connections. I then used different colors and labels to distinguish categories and relationship types, added a legend, and arranged the nodes to make the supply chain easier to follow.

4. Revisions and Decisions
I refined the layout to improve readability and clarify relationships between NVIDIA, TSMC, memory suppliers, manufacturing contractors, and cloud services. I also considered the difference between company-level supplier relationships and relationships specifically confirmed for the H100 GPU.

5. Reflection and Limitations
The visualization helped me see how AI hardware depends on an interconnected global supply chain. However, the network does not fully represent workers, labor conditions, transportation, or environmental costs. ChatGPT helped me organize data and improve the design, while I made the final decisions about the topic and visualization.

## Reflection
In my AI supply chain network, companies such as NVIDIA and TSMC are highly visible because they connect to many other companies, technologies, and stages of production. Mapping these relationships helped me understand that AI does not exist only as software. It depends on semiconductor manufacturing, memory suppliers, specialized equipment, raw materials, and cloud computing services. The network reveals power by showing which companies connect different stages and how many other parts of the supply chain depend on them.

However, the visualization also shows how easily some people and issues can become invisible. My map focuses mainly on companies and technologies, while the workers who mine materials, manufacture chips, and assemble hardware are largely absent. The environmental costs of producing AI hardware, including water and energy consumption, are also not fully represented. These absences do not mean they are unimportant; they reflect what I chose to include in my network and what information was available.

I also realized that the way a network is organized can influence how people understand power. If I centered workers, mining regions, or environmental resources instead of major corporations, the map would tell a different story. Therefore, a network visualization is not completely neutral. It makes certain relationships easier to see while potentially hiding others.
