## Reflection
With AI tools like DALL-E, the idea of "making" art is completely changing. Traditionally, creating an image meant working directly with materials-drawing lines, mixing colors, or placing pixels by hand to get a specific result. Now, the human's role is simply to prompt the idea and choose the best outcome. You "make" art by writing a clear text prompt, picking the right words, and selecting your favorite image from what the AI offers. The machine handles the physical "making" by turning your words into tokens, running them through its algorithms, and generating a picture that matches your description. This shifts the creative process from predictable, step-by-step work to a back-and-forth collaboration. The human acts more like an art director, picking the best options from an endless supply of machine-generated ideas. This dramatically lowers the barrier to entry, allowing anyone who can type to create complex artwork. However, it also complicates how we think about ownership and effort. Because the AI learned how to draw by analyzing millions of human responses of images, using it means you are essentially pulling from a massive, collective database of human art. You have to collaborate with a tool that has its own built-in habits, cultural assumptions, and biases. Ultimately, the final image is a true co-production; one where your original idea might be changed, shaped, or even completely rewritten by the AI's internal logic.
